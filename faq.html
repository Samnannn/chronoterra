Paradox FAQ

Paradox, answered.

Past selves, closed portals and the correct way to hold a souvenir.

Answers are fictional and internally consistent; the last one is the only one that is entirely true.

10 of 10 questions shown

Selves & timelines

Can I meet my past self?

Technically, yes. The Board’s gates are scheduled so that two versions of you never share an hour, but accidents happen. If you do meet, the Board’s guidance is “wave, do not speak,” followed by a firm handshake and no advice. Your past self will not take it, and you will resent that they will not.

What if I visit a timeline that never existed?

Alternate-timeline destinations, such as the Last Day of Wi-Fi, are catalogued as “Branch B.” They are real in the sense that you can walk around in them, and imaginary in the sense that they cannot be verified. Souvenirs from Branch B dissolve at the gate. We recommend memories, which travel well.

Do I age while travelling?

You age at the speed of your own days, wherever you are. If you spend a week in 1631, you are a week older when you return, even if only nine minutes have passed at home. The Board offers no refunds on this.

History & its customs

Can I change history?

The Board’s Elastic Timeline Principle holds that the past absorbs small perturbations: a dropped coin, an unwise remark. Larger changes are intercepted by the Integrity Desk, usually before you know you have made them. In short: you may be surprised which events the Board considers “large,” but you will not be surprised by the paperwork.

Can I talk to historical figures?

Listening is permitted. Telling is not. You may not mention future events, future technologies or the outcome of anything. If a famous person asks you a direct question, smile and ask them one in return. It works better than you would think.

Will anyone believe I am from the future?

Almost nobody asks. The Board’s Linen Principle applies: dress correctly, speak softly, and let the era’s own confidence carry you. The people most likely to detect a visitor are small children and cats, and neither will tell.

Time, return & souvenirs

What happens if my return portal closes?

Portals do not close; they wait. Each gate re-opens at its anchor coordinate within twenty-four local hours. If it does not, activate your Tether and follow the R.E.T.U.R.N. protocol. In the rare event of a long delay, the Board retrieves travellers within seventy-two hours. Bring a book.

Does time pass at the same rate everywhere?

No. Every era has a drift ratio between Origin Time (your home) and Local Time (the destination). Most expeditions return within minutes of departure; a few, notably Class V, do not. Always consult your Drift Table, and never promise to be back “in a minute.”

Can I bring souvenirs back?

Yes, with conditions. Class A (consumables you ate, photographs of permitted sites, memories) require no declaration. Class B (period-appropriate trinkets, bought legitimately) must be declared at the gate. Anything from a protected site, or anything that sits in a museum today, is prohibited. Yes, even that one.

Is any of this real?

No. CHRONOTERRA is a fictional tourism board created for a web-design competition. No time travel is offered, no booking is real, and nothing here is safety or historical advice. The imagined destinations are fiction, and historical context is labelled as such.

No paradox matches that. Try fewer words, or fewer centuries.
