A · The art of arriving

Fig. 1 — Three orbits, one traveller. Not to scale. Not, strictly, to physics.

Travel used to mean distance. Now it means date.

Temporal tourism begins with a simple confession: you have already seen most of the world. What you haven’t seen is when.

CHRONOTERRA arranges arrival in other centuries, carefully, respectfully and with extremely good luggage. We do not sell nostalgia and we do not sell prophecy. We sell attention: a few days of standing quietly in a moment that was, or might have been, or has not yet happened, and learning what it felt like.

Every expedition is labelled. Historical journeys are interpretive reconstructions. Speculative and alternate-timeline journeys are fiction, offered as such.

  1. 01

    Choose

    Pick a century. Our desk assigns a historian, a coordinate and a polite warning.

  2. 02

    Calibrate

    Be fitted, briefed and inoculated against nostalgia. Your return coordinate is sewn into your hem.

  3. 03

    Cross

    Walk through the gate. Behave. Return before the tea goes cold.

D · Travel advisories

Travel, within reason.

Every era has house rules. These are ours. Fictional guidance from an imaginary Board; not real safety advice.

ADV-09Fictional advisory

Do not alter events of historical significance.

The Board’s Integrity Desk monitors every itinerary. If you find yourself wondering whether a small intervention might be harmless, it is not harmless, and you are already on the list.

Temporal riskClass IV · SevereRead advisory

ADV-06Fictional advisory

Chronological jet lag is real.

Crossing centuries unsettles the body’s sense of when it is. Expect odd appetites, unwarranted nostalgia and a feeling that you have forgotten something. You have: it is called Tuesday. Drink water and keep a field note.

Temporal riskClass II · MildRead advisory

ADV-13Fictional advisory

Never accept an unverified invitation from your future self.

They sound very convincing. They know your middle name, your favourite sandwich and exactly what you want to hear. Verify via the Board’s authentication line and, if in doubt, decline politely and leave the room.
